Formula and interpretation

A mass-loading calculation relates flow rate Q and concentration C through ṁ = Q×C. This is dimensionally correct when Q is in volume/time and C is in mass/volume, giving mass/time. Convert mg/L to kg/m³ before applying SI quantities to avoid thousand-fold unit mistakes.

Worked mass-loading example

A stream has Q = 0.20 m³/s and C = 10 mg/L. Because 10 mg/L = 0.010 kg/m³, the mass-loading rate is 0.20 × 0.010 = 0.002 kg/s = 2 g/s. For a daily load, multiply by 86,400 s/day to obtain 172.8 kg/day. This is a steady-flow illustration.

Assumptions and common errors

  • Sample representativeness: an instantaneous concentration may not describe an entire day.
  • Regulatory standards: a numerical result does not establish legal discharge compliance.
  • Unit conversions: mg/L, g/m³ and kg/m³ must be handled consistently.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between concentration and load?
Concentration is mass per volume; load combines concentration with flow to give mass per time.
Can I use the formulas for a wastewater project?
They are suitable for learning and preliminary checks, not a substitute for approved project criteria and professional design.
Are the equations available in Arabic?
The library includes English and Arabic formula labels and explanations for supported entries.
